# Extract

## Claim

Fourth-century Greek lists (Cyril of Jerusalem's catecheses, Gregory of Nazianzus's poetic catalogue, Amphilochius of Iconium, and related witnesses) show strong agreement on the New Testament core, with occasional hesitation about Revelation or a few catholic epistles. Jerome (*Prologus Galeatus* and related prefaces) prefers the Hebrew Old Testament reckoning and speaks cautiously about books outside it, yet the Latin Church continued to read the deuterocanonicals in liturgy and, with Augustine and the African councils, received them as Scripture.

## Evidence

Primary: Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catecheses* 4; Gregory Nazianzen, *Carmen* on the genuine books; Amphilochius, iambic lines to Seleucus; Jerome, *Prologus Galeatus*; compared in Gallagher & Meade's Greek and Latin chapters.

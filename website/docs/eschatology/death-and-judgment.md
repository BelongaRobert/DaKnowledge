# Death and Judgment

## The end of earthly pilgrimage

Death is the separation of the soul from the body. It is a consequence of sin (Romans 5:12), and it is also the door through which the Christian passes to God. The Church does not treat death as annihilation. She treats it as a meeting.

> *"It is appointed for men to die once, and after that comes judgment."*  
> — Hebrews 9:27

---

## Particular judgment

At the moment of death, each person is judged in relation to Christ: whether the soul is in the grace of charity, or not. This is the **particular judgment**. It is not a second Gospel. It is the truth of this life made final.

The **general judgment** at the coming of Christ will not reverse the particular judgment. It will reveal it before all, when the body is raised and the whole of history is disclosed.

> *"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive good or evil, according to what he has done in the body."*  
> — 2 Corinthians 5:10

---

## The Christian meaning of dying

Baptism has already joined us to the death of Christ (Romans 6:3-4). The [Anointing of the Sick](../sacraments/anointing/) and Viaticum prepare the dying to make that union explicit. To die "in the Lord" is to let the last act be an act of faith, hope, and charity.

The Hail Mary asks for help "now and at the hour of our death" because the last hour is still an hour of freedom and of need.

---

## Remembering the dead

The Church prays for the dead. That prayer only makes sense if the story is not over at the grave, and if some of the dead can still be helped. See [Heaven, Hell, and Purgatory](last-things/).

---

    **CCC 1022:** *"Each man receives his eternal retribution in his immortal soul at the very moment of his death, in a particular judgment that refers his life to Christ."*

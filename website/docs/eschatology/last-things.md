# Heaven, Hell, and Purgatory

## The final states

After death comes not sleep without end, but a state. The Church names three in the ordinary catechesis of the last things: heaven, hell, and the purification of the saved that she calls purgatory. All three are about God. They are not three arbitrary rooms.

> *"This is eternal life, that they know you the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent."*  
> — John 17:3

---

## Heaven

Heaven is the blessed community of all who are perfectly incorporated into Christ. It is the vision of God face to face, the source of inexhaustible beatitude. It is not boredom and not a private cloud. It is communion: with the Trinity, with Mary and the saints, with every person saved.

The resurrection of the body belongs to this hope. The blessed are not complete until the last day, when the whole person is restored.

> *"Beloved, we are God's children now; it does not yet appear what we shall be, but we know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is."*  
> — 1 John 3:2

---

## Hell

Hell is the state of definitive self-exclusion from communion with God. The Church does not rejoice in it. She warns of it because love can be refused. God does not damn with delight. The damned have chosen a life without charity, and God respects that choice to the end.

The images of fire and darkness in Scripture are not to be emptied out. They name a real ruin. They also must not be turned into a map of God's cruelty. The Judge is the Crucified.

> *"Depart from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels."*  
> — Matthew 25:41

---

## Purgatory

Those who die in God's grace and friendship, but are still imperfectly purified, are indeed assured of salvation. After death they undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven (CCC 1030).

This is not a second chance for the unrepentant. It is the mercy that finishes what grace began. The Church's prayer for the dead, Masses offered for them, and almsgiving rest on this communion.

> *"It is therefore a holy and wholesome thought to pray for the dead, that they may be loosed from sins."*  
> — 2 Maccabees 12:46

---

## The new heaven and the new earth

The last word is not the fate of isolated souls. It is a renewed creation (Revelation 21:1-4). Eschatology is cosmic because the Incarnation is cosmic. The God who made the world does not discard it.

Heaven is the ultimate end and fulfillment of the deepest human longings, the state of supreme, definitive happiness."*

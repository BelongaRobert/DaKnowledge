# The Assumption

## Body and soul into glory

The Assumption is the dogma that the Immaculate Mother of God, the ever-Virgin Mary, when the course of her earthly life was finished, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory. Pope Pius XII defined it in *Munificentissimus Deus* (1950).

The Church does not define whether Mary died. She defines the end: Mary is not a soul waiting for a body. She already shares, in a unique way, the destiny promised to all the redeemed.

> *"For he has regarded the low estate of his handmaiden. For behold, henceforth all generations will call me blessed."*  
> — Luke 1:48

---

## The resurrection begun in one of us

Christ is the firstfruits (1 Corinthians 15:20). Mary is the first of the Church to share His risen life in the full integrity of the human person. The Assumption is not Mary escaping the Gospel. It is the Gospel arriving early in the woman who said yes.

This is why the dogma belongs next to [Eschatology](../eschatology/). What she is, the Church hopes to be: body and soul, with God.

> *"When Christ who is our life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory."*  
> — Colossians 3:4

---

## Dormition and Assumption

The East speaks of the Dormition, the falling asleep of the Theotokos. The West speaks of the Assumption. The two names are not two faiths. They are two ways of saying that Mary was not abandoned to corruption.

The empty tomb of Mary is not an article of the Creed. The glorification of Mary is.

---

## Queen and intercessor

Crowned in heaven, she remains a creature. Queenship in the Church's language is participation in the kingship of her Son, not a second throne. The Rosary and the liturgical memorials keep this in prayer, not in speculation.

---

!!! quote "Catechism of the Catholic Church"
    **CCC 966:** *"The Assumption of the Blessed Virgin is a singular participation in her Son's Resurrection and an anticipation of the resurrection of other Christians."*

# Immaculate Conception

## Preserved from the first moment

The Immaculate Conception is the dogma that the Blessed Virgin Mary, from the first instant of her conception, was preserved immune from all stain of original sin, by a singular grace and privilege of God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ.

It is not the virginal conception of Jesus. It is Mary's own beginning.

> *"Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you."*  
> — Luke 1:28

The angel does not greet a woman who will later become gracious. He names her as already filled with grace.

---

## Defined, not invented

Pope Pius IX defined the dogma in *Ineffabilis Deus* (1854). The definition did not create the faith of the Church. It judged a long memory: the new Eve, the all-holy, the one whom the liturgy already praised as conceived without sin.

The grace is **preventive**, not earned. Mary is redeemed more perfectly, not less. She is saved by Christ in advance of Calvary, as we are saved from Calvary. The merits are His.

> *"He chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him."*  
> — Ephesians 1:4

---

## Why this matters for Christology

A Christ who assumes flesh from a nature still under the reign of sin is not unthinkable to God. The Immaculate Conception is not a necessity imposed on God. It is a fittingness the Church has been given to see: the Ark is prepared for the Word.

It also guards the truth of [original sin](../soteriology/). If Mary is preserved, then original sin is real enough to need preservation.

---

## The dogma and the Christian life

This teaching is not a remote privilege. It shows what grace can do. Holiness is possible because God acts first. The [Hail Mary](../prayer/hail-mary/) keeps the dogma in the mouth of the poor: "full of grace."

---

!!! quote "Catechism of the Catholic Church"
    **CCC 491:** *"Through the centuries the Church has become ever more aware that Mary, 'full of grace' through God, was redeemed from the moment of her conception."*

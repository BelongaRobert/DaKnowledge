# Mother of God

## Theotokos

The title **Theotokos** (Greek: "God-bearer," Mother of God) is the Church's short way of saying that the child of Mary is the eternal Son, not a human person later joined to God. To refuse the title is to split Christ.

> *"And why is this granted me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"*  
> — Luke 1:43

Elizabeth calls Mary "the mother of my Lord." The Council of Ephesus (431) defined that this Lord is God the Word.

---

## What the title does and does not mean

Mary is not the mother of the divine nature. She is the mother of the **person** who is God. Mothers bear persons, not natures in the abstract. Because the person she bore is the Son, she is rightly called Mother of God.

This is the same mystery as the [Hypostatic Union](../christology/hypostatic-union/). Nestorius wanted to protect God by calling Mary only *Christotokos*, mother of Christ. The Church answered that a Christ who is not God in Mary's womb is not the Christ of the Gospel.

> *"The Word became flesh and dwelt among us."*  
> — John 1:14

---

## Perpetual virginity

The Church confesses Mary ever-virgin: before, during, and after the birth of Jesus. The title "brothers of the Lord" in the Gospels does not overturn that confession; the Church has read those texts in the light of her memory of Mary.

Virginity here is not a slight against marriage. It marks the uniqueness of this birth. The Son has a Father in heaven and a mother on earth. He does not have a human father in the order of generation.

---

## Spiritual motherhood

From the cross Jesus gives Mary to the beloved disciple: "Behold, your mother" (John 19:27). The Church reads this as more than a household arrangement. Mary is mother of the faithful because she is mother of the Head.

This motherhood is intercession, not replacement. There is one mediator (1 Timothy 2:5). Mary's prayer is the prayer of a creature who is wholly in that one mediation.

---

    *"If anyone does not confess that Emmanuel is truly God and that therefore the holy Virgin is Theotokos, for she gave birth in the flesh to the Word of God become flesh, let him be anathema."*  
    — Second letter of Cyril, received at Ephesus

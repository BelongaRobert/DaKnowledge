# Moral Theology

## The life that answers God

Moral theology is the study of the good life in the light of revelation. It is not a code detached from dogma. It is the human person's free answer to the God who has already acted: created, redeemed, and sanctified.

> *"You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind. This is the great and first commandment. And a second is like it, You shall love your neighbor as yourself."*  
> — Matthew 22:37-39

---

## Beatitude, not mere rule

The moral life begins with a question of happiness. The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3-12) are not decorations on the Law. They are the shape of life in the kingdom. The commandments protect that life; they do not replace it.

Catholic moral teaching holds together:

- **The dignity of the person**, made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27)
- **Freedom** that is ordered to the good, not to whim
- **Grace**, without which we cannot live the life we are shown
- **The Church**, in whose communion conscience is formed

## Grace and works

The moral life does not earn the beginning of justification. It is the fruit of grace already given. Faith without works is dead (James 2:26). Works without grace are not the Gospel. See [Justification](../soteriology/justification/) and [Sanctification](../soteriology/sanctification/).

> *"We are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them."*  
> — Ephesians 2:10

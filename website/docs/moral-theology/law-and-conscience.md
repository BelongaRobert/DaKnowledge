# Law and Conscience

## The law that is not an enemy

Law, in Catholic teaching, is an ordinance of reason for the common good, given by the one who has care of the community (St. Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologica*, I-II, q. 90, a. 4). It is not the opposite of love. It is love made intelligible.

> *"I will put my law within them, and I will write it upon their hearts."*  
> — Jeremiah 31:33

---

## Kinds of law

### Eternal law
God's wise governance of all things. Every other law participates in this.

### Natural law
The rational creature's share in the eternal law. It is not a second Bible. It is the good we can know because we are made in God's image: to preserve life, to seek truth, to live in society, to honor God. *Humanae Vitae* and the social encyclicals rest on this, not on a sectarian taste.

### Revealed law
The Decalogue and the law of the Gospel. The Ten Commandments name the natural law in the history of the covenant. The Sermon on the Mount interiorizes it.

### Human law
Civil and ecclesiastical law. These bind when they are just. They do not create the good; they apply it.

---

## Conscience

Conscience is not a private pope. It is the judgment of reason by which we recognize the moral quality of a concrete act (CCC 1778). It must be formed. An erroneous conscience binds in the moment, but we are responsible for the negligence that left it dark.

> *"When Gentiles who have not the law do by nature what the law requires, they are a law to themselves."*  
> — Romans 2:14

The Church forms conscience; she does not replace it. A Catholic does not check the Magisterium at the door of the heart. He lets the same Spirit who guards the Church teach the heart.

---

## Freedom

Freedom is the power to act or not to act, and so to perform deliberate acts (CCC 1731). It is perfected in the gift of self, not in the refusal of every claim. "For freedom Christ has set us free" (Galatians 5:1) is freedom *for* charity, not freedom *from* the good.

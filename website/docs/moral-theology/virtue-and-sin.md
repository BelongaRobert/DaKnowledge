# Virtue and Sin

## What we become

A virtue is a habitual and firm disposition to do the good (CCC 1803). Sin is a word, deed, or desire contrary to the eternal law. Moral theology is about both, because the Christian life is not a series of isolated choices. It is a nature being healed and raised.

> *"Make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue."*  
> — 2 Peter 1:5

---

## The theological virtues

These are infused. We do not give them to ourselves.

| Virtue | Act | End |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **Faith** | Believe God and believe in God | God as First Truth |
| **Hope** | Trust God's promises | God as our beatitude |
| **Charity** | Love God for His own sake and the neighbor in God | God as Friend |

Charity is the form of the virtues. Without it, the rest is a body without a soul.

---

## The cardinal virtues

Prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance order the human powers. They are named in Wisdom 8:7 and taken up by the Fathers and St. Thomas. Grace does not abolish them. It heals and elevates them.

The gifts of the Spirit, treated under [Pneumatology](../pneumatology/spiritual-gifts/), make the soul ready to be moved by God beyond the ordinary measure of the virtues.

---

## Sin

### Original and actual
[Original sin](../soteriology/) is the inherited wound. Actual sin is our own. Actual sin is **mortal** when the matter is grave, the knowledge sufficient, and the consent deliberate. It destroys charity in the heart. **Venial** sin wounds charity without destroying it.

> *"There is sin which is mortal... All wrongdoing is sin, but there is sin which is not mortal."*  
> — 1 John 5:16-17

### Capital sins
Pride, avarice, envy, wrath, lust, gluttony, and sloth are called capital because they beget other sins. They are not the only sins. They are wells.

---

## Conversion

The sacrament of [Reconciliation](../sacraments/reconciliation/) is the ordinary way back from mortal sin. Contrition, confession, and satisfaction are not a human tax. They are the shape of a wound being closed by Christ.

Virtue grows by repeated acts under grace, and by the Eucharist, which is food for the road, not a prize for the finished.

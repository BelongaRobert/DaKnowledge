# Scripture

## The Inspired Word of God

The Bible is the Word of God in the words of men: written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, entrusted to the Church, and read in the one economy of salvation that runs from Genesis to Revelation.

> *"All scripture is inspired by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness."*  
> — 2 Timothy 3:16

Catholic faith does not set Scripture against Tradition. Both flow from the same wellspring. The Church recognized the canon; she did not invent it.

## How to read Scripture here

Read the whole as one revelation. The Old Testament prepares for the New; the New unveils the Old. The literal sense is the foundation. Allegorical, moral, and anagogical senses grow from it; they do not replace it.

Private reading is good. Private reading *apart from the Church* is how texts become weapons. The same Spirit who inspired the authors guards the Church's memory of what those authors meant to affirm.

> *"The Church does not draw her certainty about all revealed truths from the holy Scriptures alone. Both Scripture and Tradition must be accepted and honored with equal sentiments of devotion and reverence."*  
> — Dei Verbum, 9

## In the life of the Church

Scripture is proclaimed at Mass, prayed in the Liturgy of the Hours, and received in lectio divina. For the prayers that grow out of that reading, see [Prayer](../prayer/). For how the Word became flesh, see [Christology](../christology/).

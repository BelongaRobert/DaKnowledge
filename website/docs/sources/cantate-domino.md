# *Cantate Domino* (4 February 1442)

Bull of union of the Copts and Ethiopians · Council of Florence, Session XI · Pope Eugene IV

## English study rendering

Eugene, bishop, servant of the servants of God, for an everlasting memorial of the matter.

“Sing to the Lord, for He has done magnificently; announce this in all the earth; exult and praise, O dwelling of Zion, for great in your midst is the Holy One of Israel” (Is 12:5–6).

Truly it is fitting that the Church of God sing and exult in the Lord for this great magnificence and glory of His name, which the most clement God has deigned to work on this present day. Indeed it becomes us to praise and bless our Savior with our whole heart, who daily heaps new increases upon His holy Church. Although His benefits toward the Christian people are at every time many and great, which show more clearly than light His immense charity toward us, nevertheless, if we carefully examine what and how great things divine clemency has deigned to do in these most recent days, we shall certainly be able to judge that in this our time there have existed more and greater gifts of His charity than in many ages gone by. For behold, not yet three years having been completed in this holy ecumenical synod, our Lord Jesus Christ, by His untiring piety, has so abundantly brought about the most salutary union of three great nations unto the common and perpetual joy of all Christendom. Whence it has come about that almost the whole East which adores the glorious name of Christ, and no small portion of the North, after long disagreements, have already come together with the holy Roman Church in the same bond of faith and charity. For first the Greeks and those who are subject to the four patriarchal sees containing many peoples and nations and languages, then the Armenians, a nation of many peoples, and today indeed the Jacobites, likewise great peoples throughout Egypt, have been united to the holy Apostolic See.

And since nothing is more pleasing to our Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, than mutual charity among men, and nothing can be more glorious to His name and more useful to the Church than that Christians, every disagreement among themselves having been removed, should come together at once in the same purity of faith, rightly ought we all both to sing for joy and to rejoice in the Lord, whom divine mercy has made worthy to see so great a magnificence of the Christian faith in our days. Therefore with the highest eagerness we announce these mighty works in the whole land of Christians, so that just as we have been filled with unspeakable joy for the glory of God and the exaltation of the Church, so also we may make others sharers of so great a gladness, that all with one mouth may magnify and glorify God (Rom 15:6) and may give great and daily thanks to His majesty, as is worthy, for so many and so wonderful benefits conferred in this age upon His holy Church. And since he who diligently exercises the work of God not only awaits merit and recompense in heaven, but also deserves ample glory and praise among men, we judge that our venerable brother John, patriarch of the Jacobites, most desirous of this holy union, is rightly to be praised and extolled by us and by the whole Church, and is worthy, together with his whole people, of the common favor of all Christians. For he, stirred by us through our orator (Albert of Sarteano, O.F.M.) and by letters to send a legation to us and to this holy synod and to unite himself and his people in the same faith with the Roman Church, destined to us and to this synod our beloved son Andrew, Egyptian by nation, abbot of the monastery of Saint Anthony in Egypt, in which Saint Anthony himself is said both to have dwelt and to have died, a man not moderately instructed in religion and morals; and inflamed with zeal of devotion he imposed and committed to him that he should reverently receive, in the name of the patriarch himself and of his Jacobites, the doctrine of the faith which the holy Roman Church holds and preaches, to be afterward carried by him to the patriarch himself and to the Jacobites, so that they also might acknowledge the same and hold it ratified and preach it in their regions.

We therefore, to whom by the voice of the Lord it has been committed to feed the sheep of Christ (cf. Jn 21:17), caused Abbot Andrew himself to be diligently examined by several distinguished men of this sacred council concerning the articles of the faith and the sacraments of the Church and whatever things look to salvation; and at length, the Catholic faith of the holy Roman Church having been set forth to the same abbot as much as seemed necessary, and having been humbly accepted by him, today in this solemn session, the sacred ecumenical Council of Florence approving, we have handed over in the name of the Lord the following true and necessary doctrine.

### The Trinity

First, therefore, the most holy Roman Church, founded by the word of our Lord and Savior, firmly believes, professes, and preaches one true God, almighty, unchangeable, and eternal; Father and Son and Holy Spirit; one in essence, threefold in persons; the Father unbegotten, the Son begotten from the Father, the Holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son; that the Father is not the Son or the Holy Spirit, that the Son is not the Father or the Holy Spirit, that the Holy Spirit is not the Father or the Son; but the Father alone is Father, the Son alone is Son, the Holy Spirit alone is Holy Spirit. The Father alone has begotten the Son from His own substance. The Son alone has been begotten from the Father alone. The Holy Spirit alone proceeds at once from the Father and the Son. These three persons are one God, not three gods, because of the three there is one substance, one essence, one nature, one divinity, one immensity, one eternity, and all things are one where opposition of relation does not stand in the way. Because of this unity the Father is wholly in the Son, wholly in the Holy Spirit; the Son is wholly in the Father, wholly in the Holy Spirit; the Holy Spirit is wholly in the Father, wholly in the Son. None either precedes another in eternity, or exceeds another in greatness, or surpasses another in power. For it is eternal and without beginning that the Son has existed from the Father, and eternal and without beginning that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. Whatever the Father is or has, He does not have from another, but from Himself, and He is principle without principle. Whatever the Son is or has, He has from the Father, and He is principle from a principle. Whatever the Holy Spirit is or has, He has from the Father and the Son together. But the Father and the Son are not two principles of the Holy Spirit, but one principle, just as the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are not three principles of the creature, but one principle.

Therefore it condemns, reproves, and anathematizes all who hold adverse and contrary opinions, and declares them to be alien from the body of Christ, which is the Church. Hence it condemns Sabellius, who confuses the persons and utterly takes away their real distinction; it condemns the Arians, Eunomians, and Macedonians, who say that only the Father is true God, but place the Son and the Holy Spirit in the order of creatures. It also condemns all others who make degrees or inequality in the Trinity.

### Creation

It most firmly believes, professes, and preaches that the one true God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, is the creator of all things visible and invisible, who, when He willed, by His goodness founded all creatures both spiritual and bodily, good indeed, because made by the highest good, but changeable, because made from nothing; and it asserts that there is no nature of evil, because every nature, insofar as it is a nature, is good.

### Scripture

It professes one and the same God as the author of the Old and of the New Testament, that is, of the Law and the Prophets and of the Gospel, since the saints of both Testaments spoke under the inspiration of the same Holy Spirit; whose books it receives and venerates, which are contained under the following titles: the five of Moses, that is Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy; Joshua, Judges, Ruth, Four of Kings, Two of Paralipomenon, Ezra, Nehemiah, Tobit, Judith, Esther, Job, Psalms of David, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom, Ecclesiasticus, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Baruch, Ezekiel, Daniel; the Twelve Minor Prophets, that is Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi; Two of Maccabees; Four Gospels, of Matthew, Mark, Luke, John; Fourteen Epistles of Paul: to the Romans, Two to the Corinthians, to the Galatians, to the Ephesians, to the Philippians, Two to the Thessalonians, to the Colossians, Two to Timothy, to Titus, to Philemon, to the Hebrews; Two of Peter; Three of John; One of James; One of Jude; the Acts of the Apostles; and the Apocalypse of John.

For this reason it anathematizes the madness of the Manichaeans, who posited two first principles, one of things visible, another of things invisible, and said that the God of the New Testament is one, and the God of the Old another.

### The Incarnation

It firmly believes, professes, and preaches that one person from the Trinity, the true God, the Son of God begotten from the Father, consubstantial and coeternal with the Father, in the fullness of time which the unsearchable depth of the divine counsel disposed, for the salvation of the human race, assumed a true and integral nature of man from the immaculate womb of the Virgin Mary and joined it to Himself into the unity of person with such unity that whatever there is of God is not separated from the man, and whatever is of man is not divided from the Godhead; and that He is one and the same, undivided, both natures remaining in their properties: God and man, Son of God and Son of man; equal to the Father according to divinity, less than the Father according to humanity (Athanasian Creed); immortal and eternal from the nature of divinity, passible and temporal from the condition of the assumed humanity.

It firmly believes, professes, and preaches that the Son of God, in the assumed humanity, was truly born from the Virgin, truly suffered, truly died and was buried, truly rose from the dead, ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father, and will come at the end of the ages to judge the living and the dead. But it anathematizes, execrates, and condemns every heresy that holds the contrary. And first it condemns Ebion, Cerinthus, Marcion, Paul of Samosata, Photinus, and all who similarly blaspheme, who, not being able to perceive the personal union of humanity to the Word, denied that Jesus Christ our Lord is true God, confessing Him a mere man who, by a greater participation of divine grace than he would have received by the merit of a holier life, would be called a divine man. It also anathematizes Mani with his followers, who, dreaming that the Son of God assumed not a true body but a fantastic one, utterly took away the truth of humanity in Christ; and likewise Valentinus, asserting that the Son of God took nothing from the Virgin Mother, but assumed a heavenly body and so passed through the womb of the Virgin as water flowing through an aqueduct runs through. Also Arius, who, asserting that the body assumed from the Virgin lacked a soul, held that the Godhead was in place of a soul. Also Apollinaris, who, understanding that if a soul informing the body is denied, true humanity would not have been there in Christ, posited only a sensitive soul, but held that the Godhead of the Word took the place of a rational soul. It also anathematizes Theodore of Mopsuestia and Nestorius, asserting that the humanity was united to the Son of God through grace, and on that account that there are two persons in Christ, just as they confess that there are two natures, since they were not able to understand that the union of humanity to the Word was hypostatic, and therefore denied that it received the subsistence of the Word. For according to this blasphemy the Word was not made flesh, but the Word through grace dwelt in flesh; that is, the Son of God was not made man, but rather the Son of God dwelt in a man. It also anathematizes, execrates, and condemns Eutyches the archimandrite, who, when he understood that according to the blasphemy of Nestorius the truth of the Incarnation is excluded and that therefore it was necessary that humanity be so united to the Word of God that of Godhead and humanity there should be one and the same person, and yet was not able to grasp the unity of person while the plurality of natures remained, just as he posited that of Godhead and humanity in Christ there is one person, so also he asserted that there is one nature, holding that before the union there was a duality of natures, but that in the assumption it passed into one nature, conceding with the greatest blasphemy and impiety that either humanity was converted into Godhead or Godhead into humanity. It also anathematizes, execrates, and condemns Macarius of Antioch and all who hold similar things, who, although he thought truly concerning the duality of natures and the unity of person, nevertheless erred enormously concerning the operations of Christ, saying that in Christ of both natures there was one operation and one will. The most holy Roman Church anathematizes all these with their heresies, affirming that in Christ there are two wills and two operations.

### Faith in Christ the Mediator

It firmly believes, professes, and teaches that no one ever conceived from man and woman has been liberated from the dominion of the devil except through faith in the mediator of God and men, Jesus Christ (cf. 1 Tim 2:5), our Lord, who, conceived, born, and dead without sin, alone by His death overthrew the enemy of the human race by blotting out our sins, and opened the entrance of the heavenly kingdom which the first man by his own sin had lost with all his succession; whom, as one day to come, all the sacred sacrifices, sacraments, and ceremonies of the Old Testament prefigured.

### Mosaic Law after the Gospel

It firmly believes, professes, and teaches that the legal prescriptions of the Old Testament or of the Mosaic law, which are divided into ceremonies, sacred sacrifices, and sacraments, because they had been instituted for the sake of signifying something future, although they were fitting for divine worship in that age, ceased when our Lord Jesus Christ, signified through them, came, and the sacraments of the New Testament began. It also teaches that anyone who after the Passion places hope in the legal prescriptions and persuades himself that they are necessary for salvation, as if the faith of Christ could not save without them, has sinned mortally. Yet it does not deny that from the Passion of Christ until the Gospel was promulgated those things could be observed, provided only that they were by no means believed necessary for salvation. But after the Gospel has been promulgated, it asserts that they cannot be observed without the destruction of eternal salvation.

Therefore it declares all observers of circumcision and of the Sabbath and of the remaining legal prescriptions after that time to be alien from the faith of Christ and by no means able to be sharers of eternal salvation, unless at some time they recover from these errors. Therefore it commands altogether all who glory in the Christian name that at whatever time, whether before or after baptism, circumcision is to be ceased from, because whether one places hope in it or not, it cannot at all be observed without the destruction of eternal salvation. But concerning children, because of the danger of death which can often occur, since help cannot be given them by any other remedy except through the sacrament of baptism, through which they are snatched from the dominion of the devil and are adopted into sons of God, it admonishes that sacred baptism is not to be deferred for forty or eighty days or some other time according to the observance of certain persons, but ought to be conferred as soon as it can conveniently be done; yet in such wise that, when the danger of death is imminent, they are to be baptized at once without any delay, even by a layman or a woman, in the form of the Church, if a priest is lacking, as is more fully contained in the decree for the Armenians.

### Foods

It firmly believes, professes, and preaches that every creature of God is good and nothing is to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving (1 Tim 4:4), because according to the word of the Lord it is not what enters into the mouth that defiles a man (Mt 15:11); and it asserts that that difference of the Mosaic law between clean and unclean foods pertains to ceremonial matters, which, when the Gospel arose, passed away and ceased to be efficacious. That prohibition also of the Apostles, from things sacrificed to idols and from blood and from what is strangled (Acts 15:29), it says was fitting for that time in which from Jews and Gentiles, who formerly lived by diverse ceremonies and customs, one Church was arising, so that with the Jews the Gentiles also might observe something in common and occasion might be given of coming together into one worship of God and one faith, and matter of dissension might be taken away, since to the Jews, because of ancient custom, blood and what is strangled seemed abominable, and by the eating of things sacrificed they could judge that the Gentiles would return to idolatry. But when the Christian religion has been so far propagated that no carnal Jew appears in it, but all who pass over to the Church come together into the same rites and ceremonies of the Gospel, believing that all things are clean to the clean (Titus 1:15), the cause of that apostolic prohibition ceasing, the effect also has ceased. Therefore it declares that no nature of food which human society admits is to be condemned, nor is distinction to be made among animals by anyone, whether man or woman, and by whatever kind of death they perish; although for the health of the body, for the exercise of virtue, for regular and ecclesiastical discipline, many things not forbidden can and ought to be left aside; because according to the Apostle all things are lawful, but not all things are expedient (1 Cor 6:12; 10:22).

### Outside the Catholic Church there is no salvation

It firmly believes, professes, and preaches that none of those existing outside the Catholic Church, not only pagans, but also neither Jews nor heretics and schismatics, can become sharers of eternal life, but that they will go into the eternal fire which has been prepared for the devil and his angels (Mt 25:41), unless before the end of life they shall have been joined to that same Church; and that the unity of the ecclesiastical body is of such value that for those alone who remain in it do the ecclesiastical sacraments profit unto salvation, and fasts, alms, and the other offices of piety and the exercises of the Christian warfare bring forth eternal rewards; and that no one, however many alms he may have done, and even if he shall have shed blood for the name of Christ, can be saved, unless he shall have remained in the bosom and unity of the Catholic Church.

### Reception of the councils

It embraces, however, approves, and receives the holy Nicene synod of three hundred eighteen Fathers, gathered in the times of the most blessed Silvester our predecessor and of the great Constantine, most pious prince, in which the impious Arian heresy with its author was condemned, and it was defined that the Son of God is consubstantial and coeternal with the Father.

It also embraces, approves, and receives the holy Constantinopolitan synod of one hundred fifty Fathers, called in the time of the most blessed Damasus our predecessor and of Theodosius the elder, which anathematized the impious error of Macedonius, who asserted that the Holy Spirit is not God but a creature. Those whom they condemn, it condemns; what they approve, it approves; and it wills that the things defined there subsist in all respects unharmed and inviolate.

It also embraces, approves, and receives the holy first Ephesine synod of two hundred Fathers, which is third in the order of universal synods, called under the most blessed Celestine our predecessor and Theodosius the younger, in which the blasphemy of the impious Nestorius was condemned, and it was defined that of our Lord Jesus Christ, true God and true man, there is one person, and that the blessed Mary ever Virgin is to be preached by the whole Church not only as *Christotokos*, but also as *Theotokos*, that is, not only mother of a man, but Mother of God.

But it condemns, anathematizes, and rejects the impious Second Ephesine synod, gathered under the most blessed Leo our predecessor and the aforesaid prince, in which Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, defender of the heresiarch Eutyches and impious persecutor of Saint Flavian, bishop of Constantinople, by craft and threats drew that execrable synod to the approval of the Eutychian impiety.

It also embraces, approves, and receives the holy Chalcedonian synod, fourth in the order of universal synods, of six hundred thirty Fathers, celebrated in the times of the aforesaid most blessed Leo our predecessor and of the prince Marcian, in which the Eutychian heresy with its author Eutyches and Dioscorus its defender was condemned, and it was defined that our Lord Jesus Christ is true God and true man,\* and that in one and the same person the divine and human natures remained integral, inviolate, incorrupt, unconfused, distinct, the humanity doing the things that are of man, and the Godhead the things that are of God. Those whom it condemns, it holds condemned; those whom it approves, approved.

\* The Holy See Latin page reads *verum Deum et verum nomine*. Sense, parallel passages in this bull, and the Holy See’s Italian version require *verum hominem* (“true man”). Treated here as a textual or typographical fault on the Latin page.

It also embraces, approves, and receives the holy fifth synod, the Second at Constantinople, celebrated in the time of the most blessed Vigilius our predecessor and of the prince Justinian, in which the definition of the sacred Council of Chalcedon concerning the two natures and one person of Christ was renewed, and many errors of Origen and of his followers, especially concerning the repentance and liberation of demons and of other damned persons, were rejected and condemned.

It also embraces, approves, and receives the holy third Constantinopolitan synod of one hundred fifty Fathers, which is sixth in the order of universal synods, gathered in the times of the most blessed Agatho our predecessor and of Constantine the Fourth of that name, prince, in which the heresy of Macarius of Antioch and of his sectaries was condemned, and it was defined that in our Lord Jesus Christ there are two perfect and integral natures and two operations, and also two wills, although there was one and the same person, to whom the actions of each nature belonged, the Godhead doing the things that are of God, and the humanity the things that are of man.

It also embraces, approves, and receives all other universal synods legitimately gathered and celebrated and confirmed by the authority of the Roman Pontiff, and especially this holy Florentine synod, in which among other things the most holy union of the Greeks and of the Armenians was completed, and many most salutary definitions concerning each union were issued, as is fully contained in the decrees promulgated thereupon, whose tenor follows in this manner:

*Laetentur caeli* ….  
*Exultate Deo* ….

### Form of consecration; bread; marriage

But because in the above-written decree for the Armenians the form of words is not set out with which the most holy Roman Church, confirmed by the doctrine and authority of the Apostles Peter and Paul, has always been accustomed to use in the consecration of the body and blood of the Lord, we have thought it right to insert it in the present document. In the consecration of the body of the Lord it uses this form of words: “For this is My body.” But of the blood: “For this is the chalice of My blood, of the new and eternal testament, the mystery of faith, which shall be poured out for you and for many unto the remission of sins” (Mt 26:28; Mk 14:18; Lk 22:10; 1 Cor 11:25).

But as for the wheaten bread in which the sacrament is confected, whether it was baked on that day or before, it matters nothing at all; for provided the substance of bread remains, it is by no means to be doubted that after the aforesaid words of the consecration of the body, pronounced by the priest with the intention of confecting, it is at once transubstantiated into the true body of Christ.

Since it is asserted that some persons reject fourth marriages as condemned, lest sin be thought to exist where it does not, since according to the Apostle, the husband being dead, the woman is released from his law and has the faculty of marrying whom she wills in the Lord (Rom 7:3; 1 Cor 7:39), and he does not distinguish whether the first, second, or third has died, we declare that not only second and third, but also fourth and further marriages can be lawfully contracted, if no canonical impediment stands in the way. Yet we say they are more commendable if, further abstaining from marriage, they remain in chastity, because just as we judge virginity to be preferred to widowhood, so also chaste widowhood to be preferred to marriages in praise and merit.

### Acceptance by Abbot Andrew

All these things having been set forth, the aforesaid Abbot Andrew, in the name of the said patriarch and in his own and that of all the Jacobites, receives and accepts with all devotion and reverence this most salutary synodal decree with all its chapters, declarations, definitions, traditions, precepts, and statutes, and every doctrine described in it, and also whatever the holy Apostolic See and the Roman Church holds and teaches. Those doctors and holy Fathers also whom the Roman Church approves, he himself reverently receives; but whatever persons and whatever the Roman Church itself reproves and condemns, he himself holds as reproved and condemned, professing as a son of true obedience, in the name aforesaid, faithfully and always to obey the ordinances and commands of that Apostolic See.

This decree having been solemnly read in the present synod in the Latin tongue, presently in Arabic, the aforesaid Abbot Andrew publicly read through there the decree of the Jacobites themselves, and added in writing in Arabic the words written below, which were immediately recited: “Most blessed Father…”

Given at Florence in a public synodal session, solemnly celebrated in the church of the house of Santa Maria Novella, at which we now reside, in the year of the Lord’s Incarnation 1441, on the day before the Nones of February, in the tenth year of our pontificate.

Given on the 4th day of February 1441, in the tenth year of the pontificate.

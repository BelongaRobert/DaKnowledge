# The Trinity

## Father, Son, and Holy Spirit

The mystery of the Holy Trinity is the central mystery of Christian faith and life. It is the mystery of God in himself: one God in three divine persons, equal in glory, undivided in majesty.

> *"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."*  
> — Matthew 28:19

The Church does not explain the Trinity as a puzzle. She confesses it as the name into which we are baptized, the communion we enter, and the God we worship.

---

## One Essence, Three Persons

There is one divine nature, not three gods. The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit are each fully God, and they are not three parts of God. What distinguishes them is relation of origin:

- The **Father** is unbegotten, the principle without principle
- The **Son** is eternally begotten of the Father
- The **Holy Spirit** eternally proceeds from the Father and the Son

This order (*taxis*) is not a ranking of worth. The three persons are co-equal and co-eternal. The external works of the Trinity are undivided: the Father creates through the Son and perfects through the Spirit.

> *"Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord."*  
> — Deuteronomy 6:4

## How to read this section

Start with the persons, then the distinction between the immanent Trinity and the economic Trinity. That distinction guards two errors at once: modalism (three masks, one person) and subordinationism (a lesser Son or Spirit).

For the divine essence shared by the three persons, see [Theology Proper](../theology-proper/). For the Son made man, see [Christology](../christology/). For the Spirit in the Church, see [Pneumatology](../pneumatology/).
